Belief vs Truth

One stored landing run. Prototype, first iteration: nothing here is simulated, and nothing is shown that the run did not record or that is not labelled as derived.

    What this run shows, in four steps

    1. What really happened

      The nozzle plane reached the ground at east and north of the pad centre, which is from it. Requirement R5 allows . The host's verdict is recorded: R5 failed.

    2. What the controller believed

      Its last estimate before contact placed the nozzle plane from the pad centre, inside the R5 circle (approximate, derived by this view). That estimate sat east of the true centre of mass, and above it in z.

    3. When they diverged

      Before the fault the estimate stayed within of the truth in x. The GNSS bias starts at (recorded event). At the first sample with the fault active the x error is already , and at the last estimate it is , close to the bias.

    4. Why it mattered

      Guidance steers the believed position to the pad. The estimator checks only the altimeter: it was rejected at (controller flag, recorded), after which guidance read the lower of the estimator and the altimeter altitude (from source code, not recorded). Nothing similar guards x and y, so the biased belief was followed there. The sensor it rejected was the one close to the truth: at the last periodic sample the altimeter is from the true nozzle altitude, while GNSS z is from the true z. At touchdown the true and the believed nozzle points are apart. This is consistent with the bias driving the miss; the run has no counterfactual, so it does not prove it.

    Click or drag on any chart, or focus the axis and use Left and Right to step one sample, Shift with an arrow for ten, Home and End for the ends, Page Up and Page Down for the previous and next event. The cursor lands only on recorded samples and no value is interpolated.

      Estimate and observations against truth

      Every strip is in error space: truth is the zero line. Estimates are lines (the controller reports one every control cycle). Observations are separate markers, never joined, because a sensor reading says nothing about what lies between two of them.

      At the cursor

      Pad plan, true scale

      Plan of the pad area at true scale. The R5 circle, the true nozzle point at touchdown and the believed nozzle point from the last estimate are drawn.

        The window shows the area around the pad at one scale for both axes. The R5 radius is the circle. The true point is the nozzle plane (the point R5 judges). The believed point is the nozzle plane as the controller's last estimate placed it, derived by this view. The GNSS marker is a centre-of-mass reading and sits at a different reference point.

        Verdicts

        Computed by the host on every step from the true state, and stored. The controller's flags are not used for any verdict.

        Mission requirements (R1 to R6)

        Mission requirements with value, limit and verdict

        All six are evaluated at touchdown. The “faults active” column is the field cause as stored. It is the same on passing and failing rows, so it records which faults were active, not which one produced a result.

        Safety invariants (S1 to S5)

        Safety invariants with worst value, limit and verdict

        Invariants keep a worst value and a first failure. They have no recorded time, so none is drawn on the time axis. They are separate from the mission requirements: the safety invariants held while R5 failed.

        Uncertainty

        U: none. This estimator holds no uncertainty. It is a fixed-gain blend with no covariance (), so there is nothing to draw as an envelope. The sensor noise settings in the case file are the simulator's noise, not the controller's belief about its own error, and are not used here as bounds.

        Limitations

        • Idealised model: contact is the nozzle plane reaching the ground, with no contact dynamics, and nothing is validated against flight data.
        • A single stored run. It shows one trajectory, not how often this happens.
        • Recorded by the earlier host. The run still verifies under the current host (see the evidence document), but it was not produced by the current build.
        • Samples every , while the controller runs every and the host checks every step. Events between two samples are drawn at their recorded times; values between samples are not shown.
        • The touchdown sample is off the sampling grid. Its truth is interpolated by the host. Its estimate and observation are the last control cycle's, not the touchdown instant's.
        • No covariance exists, so no uncertainty is shown. No counterfactual run exists, so nothing here shows what would have happened without the fault.
        • Derived quantities tagged D-view are computed by this view's extractor from recorded values and, where tagged approximate, use a proxy for terms the run does not record.
